Lilja-4-Ever (2002) 

Warning: Topic includes Child Exploitation, Rape, Human Trafficking and Suicide.


Sometimes similar art are cited as being depraved and tragic for the sake of it, either horror sensationalism or some flavorless psychopath porn. Lilja-4-Ever is not one of these, despite being one of the most uncomfortable films of all time.


Lilja-4-ever was a difficult watch, and is an extremely depressing movie to ruminate over. 


Obligatory information: 

  • This Swedish movie takes place in some former Soviet country and is Russian(language) for the most part. Released in 2002 and directed by Lukas Moodysson, the movie follows 16yo girl, Lilja after her mother abandons her, leaving her to survive alone amid poverty in a deeply cynical dump of a corner in some post-soviet bloc. 

  • This movie is based on, and is incredibly faithful to the real tragedy of Danguolė Rasalaitė, A lithuanian girl who came to Sweden in 1999, with the belief she would work as a berry picker, forced into prostitution, and took her own life at the age of 16.


There are 3 things I wish to discuss regarding this film:


  1. Lilja 


I like the word apathy here, because it reflects not just on the entire environment, but especially on Lilja herself. Lilja, is a teenager by all accounts. She's rude, abrasive, obnoxious, combative the most time, as is a teenagers' nature. She grew up with a mother until the age of 16, which appears to be a privilege in and of itself in the poverty stricken areas of the bloc. Her mother marries a man who is moving to the states, promising to bring Lilja with her, but couldn't (you think money grows on trees bro?). She eventually sends Lilja a letter saying she abandons her. (I will talk about this more a bit later)


She was frequently harassed, belittled, and was a bit of a trash can for her neighbors and "friends". This is an abandoned teenage girl that has nothing going her way, she has nothing to be thankful about, especially since her Aunt claimed the apartment (has a heater) left by Lilja's mother. 


"Go to an orphanage, Go to a friend house, or Go spread your legs out on the street like your mother did."

-Lilja's Aunt

  1. The De-stalinization of the Soviet Union

This conversation between Lilja and Volodya in the movie hit me deep, as they stare at a mural of the Red Star.


“Something happened here 50 years ago, and thats why everything sucks”.

-Lilja to Volodya


Lithuania was one of the Soviet Puppet states that was a victim of Soviet Communism. Without going into too much detail (refer to my retrospective of Come and See (1985)), the eastern bloc got really screwed for multiple decades. In 1985, after Mikhail Gorbachev loosening of the Soviet Blocs, Lithuania gained their Independence through some good old fashion revolution-ing. But the poor still suffered much after the Independence, and the apathetic and destitute environment we see in Lilja show aspects of Eastern Europe that are still recovering from Stalin and Communism. The Iron Curtain may have fallen, but Eastern Europe still bled for a while.


It is important we know the history, but,


I appreciate this movie for not being antagonistic to anything political.


"Something happened, but we have our world in front of us, and I don't need any history to know how much Humanity sucks."


I like to think this is what Lilja meant. But let this film, and by extention, the life of Rasalaitė, be recognized as the consequences of the poor political decisions made by the Soviet Union between 1930-1983.
