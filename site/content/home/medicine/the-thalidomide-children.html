The Thalidomide Tragedy

Warning: Topic includes Medical Gore and Incompetence.

We take paracetamol, tylenol, or any other OTC drugs for pain, headaches, what not.. 

We feel a sense of security with these drugs as they have gone through extensive clinical trials and testing, and the process (for the most part), remains quite bulletproof and trustworthy to the average consumer. What happens, when this system of quality control for our medication, is not reliable AT ALL?!

Normally, clinical trials in modern medicine proceeds through several (or a combination of) regulated stages: preclinical laboratory and animal testing, followed by Phase I safety testing, Phase II efficacy and dosing studies, and large Phase III controlled trials. Only after sufficient evidence of safety and effectiveness is reviewed by regulators can a drug be approved, with Phase IV monitoring continuing after release.

Rewind to shortly after WW2

Thalidomide was first developed as a tranquilizer by Swiss pharmaceutical company Ciba in 1953. The drug was abandoned in 1954 and  was acquired by German pharmaceutical company Chemie Grünenthal. (Yes the company was established by a Nazi, but that's not the point of this topic.)

Grünenthal administered thalidomide to laboratory animals and found remarkably low acute toxicity. Researchers reportedly had difficulty establishing an LD50 (the dose that kills half the test animals), which contributed to the belief that it was much safer than barbiturate sleeping drugs.

Thalidomide was released in Germany in 1957 and subsequently distributed internationally under numerous trade names.

Yeah.. That's the extent to the testing of this drugs. On Animals. Early human testing focused largely on its sedative effects and immediate toxicity, so a lot went under the hood during its release. 

The big overlook, was the drugs' affect on embryonic development in pregnant women. But even before the birth-defect connection became established, reports emerged of peripheral neuropathy in adults taking thalidomide for prolonged periods. Just making this point to show how avoidable this scandal could have been. 

Now coming to the Elephant, the Thalidomide Children. Reports emerged of children born with severely malformed or absent limbs, alongside abnormalities affecting the ears, eyes and internal organs. At first, these cases baffled physicians, who observed the sudden rise in rare congenital defects but did not know what was causing them. 

In 1961, the connection became clear independently to German physician Widukind Lenz and Australian obstetrician William McBride, who noticed that many affected pregnancies shared one thing, the mothers had taken thalidomide.
